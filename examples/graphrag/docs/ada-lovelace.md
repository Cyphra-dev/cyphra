# Ada Lovelace

## Early life

Ada Lovelace was born in London in 1815, the daughter of the poet Lord Byron. Her mother had her tutored in mathematics and science. As a young woman she met Charles Babbage and saw a demonstration model of the Difference Engine, and the two corresponded for years.

## The translation and its notes

In 1842 the Italian engineer Luigi Menabrea published a paper in French describing the Analytical Engine. Ada Lovelace translated it into English and added a series of notes that ended up three times longer than the paper itself. The notes were published in 1843 under her initials, A.A.L.

## Note G and the Bernoulli numbers

The last of her notes, Note G, describes step by step how the Analytical Engine could compute the Bernoulli numbers. It is often called the first published computer program. In the same notes she argued that the engine could act on symbols other than numbers, such as musical notes, and so could do far more than arithmetic.
