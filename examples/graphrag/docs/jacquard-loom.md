# The Jacquard loom

## Weaving patterns with cards

Joseph Marie Jacquard, a weaver from Lyon, demonstrated his loom in 1804. A chain of punched cards controlled which warp threads were lifted for each pass of the shuttle, so a single weaver could produce complex patterns such as brocade and damask in silk without a helper.

## A stored sequence of instructions

Each card encoded one row of the pattern: a hole let a hook through, a solid spot blocked it. Changing the cards changed the fabric without changing the machine. This idea of a replaceable sequence of instructions inspired Charles Babbage, who planned to feed the Analytical Engine with punched cards in the same way.

## Reaction in Lyon

The loom put many skilled silk workers out of work, and some of them attacked the machines. Within a few decades, though, thousands of Jacquard looms were running in France, and the attachment is still used for patterned weaving today.
