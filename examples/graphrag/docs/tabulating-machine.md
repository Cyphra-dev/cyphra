# The tabulating machine

## The 1890 census

The 1880 United States census took almost eight years to count by hand. For the 1890 census, the Census Office used the tabulating machine invented by Herman Hollerith. Each person was recorded on a punched card, and the machine read the holes electrically: a pin passing through a hole closed a circuit and advanced a counter.

## From cards to companies

Hollerith had seen how railway conductors punched tickets, and he credited that, rather than the Jacquard loom, for his card format. He founded the Tabulating Machine Company in 1896. Through a merger in 1911 it became part of the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company, which was renamed IBM in 1924.

## Legacy

Punched cards stayed the main way to enter data and programs into computers until the 1970s. The rectangular card with rounded corners that IBM standardised in 1928 held 80 columns, which is why many terminals still default to 80 characters per line.
