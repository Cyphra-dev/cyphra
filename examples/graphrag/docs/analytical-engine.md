# The Analytical Engine

## The Difference Engine

Charles Babbage was an English mathematician who wanted to remove human error from printed mathematical tables. In 1822 he proposed the Difference Engine, a machine that would compute polynomial values by the method of finite differences and print the results. The British government funded the work, but the engine was never finished in his lifetime: the parts demanded more precision than workshops of the time could deliver.

## A general-purpose design

From 1834 Babbage worked on a far more ambitious machine, the Analytical Engine. It separated the store, which held numbers, from the mill, which did the arithmetic: the same split as memory and processor in a modern computer. Programs and data were given to the engine on punched cards, an idea Babbage took from the Jacquard loom.

## Conditional branching

The Analytical Engine could choose what to do next based on the result of a calculation, and it could repeat a sequence of cards. With branching and loops it was, on paper, a general-purpose computer. Only a small part of the mill was ever built, and Henry Prevost Babbage, his son, assembled a fragment after his death.
